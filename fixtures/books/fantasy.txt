The tide was a common road until the levy syndicate began measuring it as property. Surveyor Iven watched the pumps at Knotmarket fail under a tariff nobody had voted for. The drowned registry held the last communal ledger. Mara, who mended pumps without asking a name, warned that the old metering warden still obeyed claims from a vanished age. Iven's lens could shift a little water, but each working blurred a memory. They would have to choose which debts a town ought to remember, and which gates it could afford to open.
